# Candidate sparring — Invent a Word We Need

Judge brief (from the kit's competition.yaml, in full): a lexicographer
picks the coinage it would most want in a dictionary. Criteria, most
important first: (1) a freshly invented word with a one-line definition;
(2) fills a real gap, instantly recognised; (3) definition exact and
self-contained, lands in one reading; (4) sayable, spellable, memorable,
sounds as though English could have grown it; (5) fresh and surprising.
No ties; margin 1 (by a hair) to 5 (overwhelmingly better).

## Candidates (all ≤ 140 chars)

- A. thresholdnesia (n.) — walking through a doorway and forgetting why you went there. (82)
- B. bedgravity (n.) — the pull that keeps you in bed five minutes past the alarm, every morning. (89)
- C. ghostread (n.) — reading a message, deciding to answer later, and never answering. (76)
- D. preguilt (n.) — feeling guilty about something you haven't done yet but probably will. (81)
- E. tabnesia (n.) — opening a browser tab and forgetting why before it loads. (70)
- F. snackrifice (n.) — giving up the last bite so someone else can have it. (68)

Screened out before sparring: yawntagion (gap already named: "contagious

## Winner: A — thresholdnesia

Refinement notes: "walking through a doorway" kept over "walking into a
room" — the doorway is the trigger (the documented doorway effect), and
the precision makes the definition land in one reading. "(n.)" kept: it
reads as a dictionary entry, which is what the judge wants in a dictionary.
Rejected variant "the amnesia of walking through a doorway..." — redundant,
the word already carries "-nesia".
